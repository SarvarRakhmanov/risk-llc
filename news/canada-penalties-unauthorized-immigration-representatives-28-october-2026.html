Canada from 28 October 2026: fines up to CAD 1.5 million for unauthorized or dishonest immigration representatives

November 2, 2026 · On October 28, 2026 IRCC announced that new monetary penalties are in force for anyone who gives paid immigration or citizenship advice or representation and breaks the rules: CAD 5,000 for working without authorization, CAD 15,000 for false or misleading information, up to CAD 1.5 million in total. The rules apply worldwide, and penalized names will be published.

What changed (official, in force): on October 28, 2026 Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) announced that new monetary penalties are now in force for people who break the law while giving paid immigration or citizenship advice or representation. They apply to licensed consultants, lawyers and other authorized representatives, and to anyone working without authorization. The final regulations appear in the Canada Gazette, Part II on November 4, 2026; the draft was published on December 21, 2024.

The amounts: CAD 5,000 for paid advice or representation without authorization; CAD 15,000 for knowingly giving false or misleading information on an applicant's behalf, or helping the applicant do so; CAD 10,000 for refusing to hand over documents during an inspection. Another CAD 15,000 is added if the misrepresentation caused an error such as an approval, plus any money the representative received; repeat findings multiply the total. Several violations can add up to CAD 1.5 million per notice.

Applies worldwide, names published: IRCC says the rules apply "no matter where you are located". A representative outside Canada can still be penalized and named. IRCC will publish the name, business, type of violation, amount and whether it was paid; the public registry is expected in 2027. Deadlines: 30 days to answer a preliminary notice, then 30 days to pay or request a review.

Who may take money for Canadian immigration work: only members in good standing of the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants, of a provincial or territorial law society or the Chambre des notaires du Québec, or Ontario paralegals licensed by the Law Society of Ontario. IRCC's advice: check the register before you pay. A paid representative must be declared on form IMM 5476.

Why this matters in Uzbekistan: agents who promise a Canadian work permit "with guarantee" and enter false job offers, bank statements or travel history into your application put you at risk. Misrepresentation can bring a refusal and a five-year ban from Canada for the applicant, and now a heavy fine for the agent. You sign the application; you answer for every line.
