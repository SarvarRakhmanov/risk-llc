Canada's 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan: 380,000 PRs a year, 220,000 new workers, up to 150,000 students

November 9, 2026 · On November 5, 2026 IRCC published the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan: 380,000 new permanent residents and 370,000 new temporary residents (220,000 workers, up to 150,000 students) in each year. A one-time measure in 2027 will help up to 200,000 people already in Canada become permanent residents.

What was announced (official plan): on November 5, 2026 Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) published the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan. The targets are the same for each of the three years. Canada plans 380,000 permanent resident admissions and 370,000 new temporary resident arrivals a year. IRCC says the figures match what the previous plan promised.

Temporary residents: 220,000 new workers and up to 150,000 new international students in each of 2027, 2028 and 2029. Canada still aims to bring down its temporary resident population, so getting a study or work permit stays competitive.

Permanent residents: economic immigration will make up more than 64% of admissions. Permanent resident admissions are to stay below 1% of Canada's population beyond 2027. Admissions rise for the Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) and the Atlantic Immigration Program. The Rural Community Immigration Pilot becomes permanent. French-speaking admissions outside Quebec rise to 9.5% in 2027, 10.5% in 2028 and 12% in 2029.

One-time measure in 2027: extra space for up to 200,000 people already living in Canada to become permanent residents. Up to 100,000 workers and their families will go through increased PNP allocations. Provinces can also select people already in the Atlantic Immigration Program and in closed programs such as Start-up Visa, Self-Employed Persons, the Agri-Food Pilot and home care worker programs. A second group of up to 100,000 covers humanitarian cases and in-Canada spouses, partners and children. IRCC aims to bring spousal sponsorship waits outside Quebec closer to 12 months.

What it means for you: if you already work in Canada, watch your province's nominee draws in 2027. If you plan to come as a student or worker, your file must be strong, because the number of new permits is capped. IRCC also scheduled an announcement on rural and remote talent for November 9, 2026, and we will report it once official details appear.
