USA: no more automatic EAD extensions, DHS final rule in force from 14 October 2026

October 21, 2026 · On 14 October 2026 DHS published a final rule confirming that work permits (EADs) are no longer automatically extended while a renewal is pending. Renewals filed on or after 30 October 2025 get no extension: the card expires on its printed date. File the renewal as soon as you are within 180 days of expiry.

Final (official): on October 14, 2026 the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) published a final rule in the Federal Register that ends the automatic extension of Employment Authorization Documents (EADs, Form I-766). The rule took effect the same day and adopts, without changes, the interim final rule of October 30, 2025. DHS received 653 public comments and kept the policy.

What it means in practice: if you filed a renewal application (Form I-765) on or after October 30, 2025, your current EAD is valid only until the date printed on the card. There is no 540-day grace period while USCIS processes the renewal. If the new card is not approved by that date, you must stop working until it arrives, and your employer must take you off work. Renewals filed before October 30, 2025 keep the old automatic extension of up to 540 days.

Who is affected: the categories that used to get the automatic extension, for example adjustment of status applicants waiting for a green card (category C09), H-4 spouses of H-1B workers (C26), spouses of E and L-1 visa holders (A17 and A18) and several humanitarian categories. The rule does not touch EADs that were already automatically extended before October 30, 2025, and it keeps the separate extensions set by law or by Federal Register notices for Temporary Protected Status (TPS). H-1B or L-1 principals, whose status itself allows work, do not use an EAD.

What USCIS advises: file the renewal as soon as your EAD is within 180 days of expiry. USCIS confirms it will not reject an eligible renewal only because it was filed earlier. Send complete evidence with the application so a Request for Evidence does not add months, and watch current processing times on the USCIS website. DHS itself admits that some dependents and green-card applicants may face a temporary gap in work authorization.

For families from Uzbekistan in the US: check the 'Card Expires' date on every EAD in your household today and count back 180 days. If you are inside that window, prepare the I-765 now. If your spouse works on an H-4 EAD or you are waiting for a green card inside the US (adjustment of status), this rule is about you. Questions about your timeline or documents?
